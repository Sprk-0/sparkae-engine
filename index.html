Why deterministic matters. Why a CUI program can use it at all.
Cloud-native tools cannot follow you inside the boundary. This one does not need to.
Deterministic
No wall-clock in the verdict path, no randomness, no model drift. The same evidence, catalog, ruleset and assessment date yield the same determinations on any machine, on any day — and every run emits a reproducibility receipt naming those inputs and the resulting verdict digest. Exports derive their identifiers and timestamps from that receipt, so they are byte-identical too.
Zero data out
The default install makes no outbound calls: no HTTP, no DNS, no CDN, no telemetry. CUI never transits a vendor cloud because there is none in the path. Nothing leaves the network unless you turn that on. Whether a given deployment is suitable for CUI still depends on how you deploy and accredit it: the software supports the boundary, it does not confer the authorization.
Satisfied has to clear published floors
A Satisfied verdict requires all seven gates to pass — evidence above the retrieval threshold, concept coverage at or above 40%, traceable references, resolved ODPs, no contradiction, temporal currency — and those thresholds are constants hashed into the ruleset digest, so the bar is published with every result. The floors are lexical: retrieval matches words, not subject matter, and evidence that clears them can still fail to address the objective. Every determination is a draft for an assessor to adopt, override or reject.
Machine-readable
Native OSCAL 1.1.2 assessment-results and POA&M, validated against the vendored NIST 1.1.2 schema on every build. Structured from the start, whatever shape the mandate takes.

This site loads no trackers, no analytics, and no third-party scripts or fonts. The engine, by default, makes no outbound calls. The browser demo assesses the package on your machine and uploads nothing.

What a pilot has to prove. Success criteria, not benchmarks.
We do not publish customer results yet. These are the measures a 30-day pilot is scored against — on your package, in your environment, with your reviewers keeping the numbers.
Coverage
Every objective in the baseline receives a verdict with a citation: 981 at Low, 1,307 at Moderate, 1,429 at High. Nothing sampled, nothing skipped.
Blockers surfaced before submission
Other-Than-Satisfied objectives ranked by gap type, each with the passage and the correction — compared against what your reviewers find by hand.
Rerun after a document change
A full rerun on the revised package, and a diff of what changed, measured in minutes on your hardware.
First-pass Examine labor
Senior-reviewer hours on the Examine pass, before and during the pilot. You keep the timesheet; the pilot report states the delta you measured.
Reproducibility
The same package produces the same verdicts on a second machine on a different day, checked by hashing the assessment-results export.
Fit with your stack
Findings land in the tool you already run (ServiceNow, RegScale, Archer, Jira) as JSON, CSV, or OSCAL, without a second system of record.

The engine. Not a GRC platform.
The commercial server product is reviewed and tested in the private product repository. Those reviews and that suite are not published here and are not claimed as a number. The public reference build carries its own conformance suite — schema validation, catalog counts, a determinism golden, a headless-browser run — on public CI.
Catalog
NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5 with 800-53A assessment objectives (447 controls, 1,513 objectives in the full catalog); FedRAMP Rev5 baselines and CORE controls in the FedRAMP profile.
Analysis
Ten local services: BM25 retrieval, concept coverage, evidence strength, defensibility, contradiction detection, temporal coherence, ODP lifecycle, gap engine (25 types), confidence, citations.
Exports
OSCAL JSON (assessment-results, POA&M), CSV; SAR and SAP (JSON + DOCX), RET, CRM, CIS, TCW in the FedRAMP profile — 14+ formats.
Intake
Nessus, ZAP and generic CSV scans; pen-test reports; vulnerabilities mapped to controls for POA&M generation.
Access control
Eight assessment roles from system admin to authorizing official, multi-tenant org isolation, permission-gated API.
Audit
Hash-chained append-only log (AU-2 / AU-3, integrity per AU-9); syslog RFC 5424 and ArcSight CEF exports.
Interfaces
The same engine behind the assessor UI and the GRC API; JSON, CSV or OSCAL into the GRC tool you already run.
Deployment
Production-hardened Docker: non-root, read-only filesystem, cap_drop ALL, health checks, graceful drain.

SparkAE is independence-preserving assessment infrastructure. Where the target is FedRAMP it is not a FedRAMP Recognized independent assessment service (historically, a 3PAO), it does not replace one, and it does not make the authorization decision or issue the official Security Assessment Report or an Authorization to Operate. The recognized assessment service controls scope, testing, conclusions, overrides, and the final deliverables; the authorizing official makes the authorization decision.
