How It Works
Upload. Assess. Export. Minutes, not weeks.
The same 7-gate evaluation pipeline a senior assessor follows — automated, deterministic, and reproducible. Ten local analytical services, zero external calls.
1

Upload Authorization Package

SSP, policies, POA&M, scan results, pen-test reports — PDF, DOCX, XLSX, CSV, OSCAL JSON. Parsed, chunked, and indexed via BM25 (or ChromaDB RAG).

2

Select Baseline & Controls

Low (156 controls / 981 DIFs), Moderate (323 / 1,307), or High (410 / 1,429). Filter by family or pick individual controls.

3

7-Gate Assessment Engine

Evidence presence → Concept coverage → Evidence strength → ODP validation → Contradiction check → Temporal coherence → Status determination with confidence + defensibility scores.

4

Review Findings

Per-DIF Satisfied / Other Than Satisfied verdicts with evidence citations, confidence scores (0–1.0), defensibility grades (A–F), concept coverage, gap taxonomy (25 types), and remediation guidance.

5

Export Draft Deliverables

OSCAL JSON (assessment-results, POA&M), SAR (JSON + DOCX), SAP, RET CSV, POA&M, CRM, CIS, TCW — 14+ formats for assessor review, provider remediation, or GRC re-ingest. Adoption into a submission package is the recognized assessment service's call, not the generator's.

Why Independence Matters
Neither side built it. Neither side controls it.

An assessment service building its own tooling creates a conflict of interest. A provider's self-assessment tool lacks credibility. SparkAE is neither party's instrument — the same engine, the same rules, applied consistently, with every determination traced to the evidence that produced it.

When the provider and the independent assessment service work in the same system, findings become comparable and attributable. The assessor sees what the provider claimed, what the evidence supports, what was remediated, and what is still open — and records their own conclusion separately from the provider's.

Machine-readable authorization data

FedRAMP is moving authorization packages toward machine-readable form. RFC-0024, which first proposed the mandate, was closed — its requirements were modified and folded into the 2026 Consolidated Rules rather than adopted as drafted, so treat the specifics as still settling. SparkAE exports native OSCAL 1.1.2 assessment-results and POA&M today, which is the part that does not change: the data is structured from the start, whatever shape the final requirement takes.
